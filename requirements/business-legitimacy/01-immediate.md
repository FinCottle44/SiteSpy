## 7. Founders / IP Agreement (⚡ NEW — HIGH PRIORITY)

Others want to join the business. **Before anyone contributes work, code, capital,
or touches client data**, get the ownership and IP position in writing. This is now
the single most urgent legal item — an undocumented "co-founder" who later claims a
share of the business or the SiteSpy IP is one of the most common ways an early
venture falls apart.

- [ ] Agree and document the ownership split (who gets what %, and on what basis)
- [ ] IP assignment: everything each person creates for the business (code, designs, docs) belongs to the business, not the individual
- [ ] Confidentiality obligations
- [ ] Vesting / leaver terms: what happens to someone's stake if they leave early
- [ ] Decision-making and how disputes are resolved

**Where to get it:**
- **SeedLegals** (seedlegals.com) — UK-specific, built for exactly this: founder agreements, equity splits, and vesting. Good if the split is non-trivial.
- **Docue** (docue.com) or **Rocket Lawyer** (rocketlawyer.com) — cheaper template-based founders/IP assignment agreements.
- **Solicitor review** — if real money or meaningful equity is involved, have a tech/startup solicitor review the final version (~£300–500). Cheaper than an ownership dispute.

**Note:** A founders agreement can be signed now even as a sole trader, then rolled
into a shareholders' agreement when you incorporate. Don't wait for the Ltd to
document who owns what.
